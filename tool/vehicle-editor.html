Bones
Automatic boxes
A set of boxes built off the model's own cubes: grouped by bone, cut wherever there is daylight straight through a group, and merged back together while merging is all but free. A draft to fit by hand — not a finished set.
Follow the model works the other way about: every cube starts as its own box and the cheapest pair is joined, over and over, until only the budget is left. It takes no notice of where one bone ends and the next begins, so it suits a machine whose bones are coarse. Trace the outline goes further: the model is burnt into cells, the cells are swept up into fine boxes, and the boxes are joined back together at whichever size and angle holds each pair tightest — the angles read off the outline itself, seen square-on from the six sides, and off the cubes' own edges. Each finished box is then fitted to the cubes it owns, whole, so a wing comes out a plate at its own sweep and thickness rather than a slab of cells.

Armament
What a machine carries, which is not in the machine's file at all: a weapon/ — what it fires and how that flies — a rack/, the carriage a store hangs off, and an equipment/ pod. One file at a time, picked out of the folder or started from nothing, and written back the same way a machine is.
